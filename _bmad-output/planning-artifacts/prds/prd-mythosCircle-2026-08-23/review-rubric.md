# PRD Quality Review — mythosCircle (Living-World NPC & World Generator for TTRPG DMs)

## Overall verdict
Strategically this PRD holds up: a sharp thesis (DMs want a living, interconnected world without the hand-crafting burden), product-specific NFRs with real bounds, an addendum that honestly maps the competitive landscape and even corrects the PRD's own overbroad positioning claim, and success metrics that validate the "living world" wedge rather than measure vanity activity. It is decision-ready for a green-light conversation, and its trade-offs are named with what was given up. What's at risk is the downstream: there are no acceptance criteria, no glossary, and no cross-referencing FR/UJ/SM IDs, so story creation would have to invent most of the "done" conditions before it can build. Close the done-ness and glossary gaps and this is a solid launch PRD.

## Decision-readiness — strong
The PRD makes decisions rather than burying them as considerations. Trade-offs are stated with their cost: local-first compute is chosen for "zero per-token cost at MVP," and the PRD names what's given up — "local model quality and throughput are the trade-off" (§4.1). Pricing is a decision with a guardrail, not a maybe: "No subscription by design — reconsider only if customers demand it. No lifetime buy: LLM usage is ongoing, so pricing must be per-usage" (§5). The DM-control guardrail is explicit and load-bearing: "This is a tool for the DM, not an AI DM. The DM always keeps the wheel" (§1). Deferrals are reasoned, not silent: Foundry is deferred because "owner doesn't use it and it's a paid license — revisit on customer demand" (§3E), and Simulate History's long-horizon ambition "stays a Later item" for a stated reason — "No LLM-driven 'what would happen' free-for-all — that's how hallucinations compound" (§3G).

The open items in §8 are genuinely open, not rhetorical: "Accounts & campaign sharing — private campaign vs. party-shared. Unresolved," "per-operation prices open," and "LLM / image / video endpoint selection on OpenRouter — TBD." That is honest. The one weakness is labeling: the §8 header reads "Open items *(carried from coaching; resolved at Finalize)*," but every item beneath it is still open — a decision-maker skimming the header could read these as settled and under-weight the one that blocks Phase 1 (accounts & campaign sharing).

### Findings
- **[medium] Open items mislabeled as resolved (§8 header)** — "resolved at Finalize" contradicts the four items beneath it, two of which are explicitly "Unresolved"/"TBD." This is a real tension being smoothed by a header. *Fix:* rename to "Open items (to resolve at/after Finalize)" and add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` on Accounts & campaign sharing, since it gates Phase 1 scope (single-user vs. party-shared changes the data model).

## Substance over theater — strong
The substance is earned. There is exactly one persona — Wren — and she drives the entire §2 journey, every capability, and the NFR example ("Wren keeps prepping the next NPC while a portrait renders," §4.5); there is no persona padding to look thorough. The NFRs are product-specific with real bounds, not boilerplate: "hundreds of entities, thousands of edges," "zero per-token cost at MVP," "deleting a campaign actually deletes it," and a content-capability NFR tied to the actual use case ("grimdark campaign content … generates without refusal," §4.3). The competitive section is the strongest evidence against theater: the addendum maps the full landscape with sources, then candidly concedes the PRD's absolute claim is overbroad — "The claim 'no tool simulates history over time' is also false in open source" — before narrowing to the defensible wedge. A PRD that pre-empts its own overclaim is doing the work, not performing it.

### Findings
- **[medium] Positioning claim broader than the evidence (§1 "What's different")** — §1 asserts "No hosted, DM-first product combines both into a living, interconnected world … lets the world simulate what happens over time," an absolute negative the addendum's own table partly undercuts (LoreKeeper has most of these attributes) and its wedge verdict explicitly narrows. *Fix:* adopt the addendum's hedged, narrower wedge in §1 ("no commercial occupant" of the specific combination: hosted + DM-first + explicit relationship graph + time simulation + image/video), so a skeptical reader isn't handed a claim the PRD's own research contradicts.

## Strategic coherence — strong
There is a thesis and every feature serves it: the bet is that DMs' real cost is hand-crafting connected NPCs, so the product generates entities that are "connected to the world already being run" (§1) and makes the payoff visible (graph, §3D) and usable (export, §3E). The phase arc is a story, not a backlog: Phase 1 establishes the core loop (world state → generation → export), Phase 2 surfaces the relationship (the graph visualizer), Phase 3 closes the loop (session log → world reaction). Nothing is ordered by ease; "See the web" is Phase 2 only because the graph model "already exists from Phase 1" (§3D) and needs a surface, not because it is quick.

Critically, the success metrics validate the thesis instead of measuring activity: the wedge metric is "% of generated entities that receive at least one *manual* relation edit; % of entities actually exported to a VTT. The two metrics that prove or kill the positioning" (§6.3) — a DM who generates 50 NPCs but never wires one relation and never exports one has validated nothing. Counter-metrics are named and meaningful ("Regenerate rate — high full/partial regeneration means generation is missing the mark," "Premium-video share — if almost nobody takes the premium tier, the layering is theater"), which is exactly what the rubric asks for. The MVP scope is coherent (an experience/productivity kind) and matches the thesis.

### Findings
None.

## Done-ness clarity — thin
This is the weakest dimension, and it is the one story creation leans on hardest. §3 is a capabilities list, not a set of verifiable requirements: no capability has an acceptance condition, and there is no Acceptance section anywhere. Several Phase 1 items are a single line with no done-boundary: "Portrait image for a generated entity" (§3C) — no resolution, format, or quality bar; "Short video (the BBEG reveal slide)" (§3C) — no duration, tier, or success bar; "Plain-language ask + world state → 2–3 candidates" (§3B) — is 2 acceptable, and which fields are mandatory in the "secret / rumor / party hook triple"? "Node-link visualizer around any entity" (§3D) — no minimum node count or interaction. "State machine computes deltas from logged actions" (§3G) — no definition of states, inputs, or output. An experienced engineer could infer most of these, but each inference is a judgment call the PRD silently delegates — the opposite of "would an engineer know what done looks like."

The NFRs are better — several carry bounds — but a key trade-off is unbound: §4.1 names "local model quality and throughput are the trade-off" without saying how much degradation or what throughput floor is acceptable, so "done" for local generation is undefined. §4.7's "hundreds of entities, thousands of edges" is a good bound but is not tied to a latency/quality target, so it does not tell an engineer when scale is "enough."

### Findings
- **[high] No acceptance criteria for any capability (§3, all of Phase 1)** — Every Phase 1 capability (world state, entity generation, media, export) is a bullet with no verifiable done condition; "2–3 candidates," "portrait image," "short video," "node-link visualizer," and "state machine computes deltas" each leave multiple material judgment calls open. Story creation cannot extract done-definitions. *Fix:* add an Acceptance subsection (or per-capability "done when" lines) for each Phase 1 capability — e.g., a candidate is done when it has name/role/personality/lore-connections/secret-rumor-hook and ≥1 relation; a portrait is done at a named resolution/format; the graph shows ≥N nodes with add/edit/delete on any edge.
- **[medium] Local-compute trade-off unbound (§4.1)** — "local model quality and throughput are the trade-off" names a cost without a floor, so acceptable local generation quality/latency is undefined. *Fix:* attach a numeric floor (e.g., "generate one NPC in <X s on the named model with Y entities in the world") so local-first "done" is testable.

## Scope honesty — adequate
Omissions are largely explicit, which is what this dimension asks for. §7 cleanly partitions Phase 1 / Phase 2 / Phase 3 / Later, and deferrals are reasoned in-line rather than silent: Roll20 is Phase 2, Foundry is deferred ("owner cost/usage constraint," §7; "owner doesn't use it and it's a paid license — revisit on customer demand," §3E), import connectors (Obsidian, World Anvil, Kanka) and Pathfinder are Later, and long-horizon simulation ("what would happen in x years") is explicitly held back from Simulate History (§3G scope note). The one non-goal that could be silently assumed — the tool is *not* an AI DM — is called out up front as a "Guardrail" (§1), which is strong.

The gap is the machinery of honesty: there are no `[ASSUMPTION: …]` tags and no Assumptions Index, so several load-bearing inferences are carried without being flagged. What "5e-ready" includes (which stat fields) is never defined; the MVP running on a single local server (effectively one operator) is assumed rather than stated; "a few invited DMs" in the closed beta (§5) is an unbounded beta size; and "the graph model already exists from Phase 1" (§3D) reads like a fact but is really a build decision that story creation will need to confirm. Open-items density is low (4 items), which is appropriate for these stakes.

### Findings
- **[medium] Load-bearing assumptions untagged (§1, §3D, §5)** — "5e-ready" (fields), single-local-server / one-operator MVP, the closed-beta size ("a few invited DMs"), and "the graph model already exists from Phase 1" are all inferences the reader must make; none are tagged. *Fix:* add `[ASSUMPTION: …]` tags inline and an Assumptions Index; promote "5e-ready" to a one-line field definition (it appears three times and is never defined).

## Downstream usability — thin
This PRD is chain-top — it feeds story creation — so this dimension matters. The cross-references that exist do resolve (§4 and §5 are cited and point to the right sections; `addendum.md` is referenced and present), so nothing is broken. But extractability is thin: there is no glossary, and the core domain nouns — entity, node, edge, candidate, world state, campaign, session, VTT, 5e-ready, and the "secret / rumor / party hook triple" — are used consistently across §1–§6 but never defined. Consistent usage helps, but a downstream agent still has to reverse-engineer each term, and "5e-ready" in particular has no referent. There are also no cross-referencing FR/UJ/SM IDs: capabilities are lettered A–G, NFRs 1–8, SMs 1–5, counter-metrics 1–4, but none of these IDs link sections to one another, so a story cannot say "implements FR B3 / validates SM 3" — it can only say "§3B."

### Findings
- **[medium] No glossary; core domain nouns undefined (§1–§6)** — entity, node, edge, candidate, world state, campaign, session, 5e-ready, and the secret/rumor/hook triple are used throughout but never defined, so every downstream workflow must infer them. *Fix:* add a short Glossary; define "5e-ready" as a concrete field list.
- **[low] No cross-referencing FR/UJ/SM IDs (§3, §6)** — Letters/numbers exist within each section but do not link sections, so story creation cannot trace a capability to the metric it validates. *Fix:* assign stable IDs (FR-…, SM-…) and cross-reference them (e.g., "FR-B3 → SM-3").

## Shape fit — adequate
The product is a consumer-facing tool with real UX, and the rubric's default for that shape is UJs with named protagonists. The PRD substitutes a single continuous Wren journey (§2) for discrete UJs — nine beats that read well and map one-to-one onto capabilities and phases. For a product with a tightly-defined core loop (build world → ask → pick/tweak → export), that narrative shape is a reasonable fit and keeps the DM as the constant operator, which is true to the product (it is, in effect, a single-operator tool even though it is consumer-branded). The cost is that no individual user journey is isolated with its own protagonist and context, so the "party-shared campaign" scenario (the open §8 item) has no journey at all — the shape quietly assumes single-DM, which is fine if that is the MVP decision but worth making explicit. Success metrics are user-facing and appropriate to the shape; nothing is over-formalized.

### Findings
- **[low] Single narrative journey assumes single-DM (§2)** — The Wren journey never exercises the party-shared scenario left open in §8, so the shape bakes in a single-operator assumption. *Fix:* either resolve Accounts & sharing to single-DM for MVP (and say so) or add a second short journey for the shared-campaign case.
