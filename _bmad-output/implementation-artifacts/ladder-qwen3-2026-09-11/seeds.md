## Characters

1. Harbormaster Ilsa Vane — takes bribes in fresh water, which is worth more than gold since the wells went brackish.
2. Sahuagin defector called Ssketh — begs sanctuary in a rain barrel, claims the Deep Maw is eating its own priest caste.
3. Tidecaller Wren — a drowned-god priestess who reads the future in flotsam; her prophecies always cost someone a finger.
4. Captain Harlow — smuggler queen of the Drowned Rat; runs blackwater gin and escaped debtors through the reef at night.
5. The Salt Abbess — runs a hospice where the dying are pickled alive "for the lean years"; half the city fears her, half owes her.
6. Bosun Cobb — lost his crew to sahuagin nets, now hires landsmen for "revenge voyages" that never return.
7. Mirella the Eel — fence for drowned jewelry; can tell which corpses wore it by smell alone.
8. Father Drown — street preacher who baptizes converts by holding them under harbor water until they stop struggling.
9. Scribe Pell — keeps the official drowning registry; for a fee, the drowned stay "alive" and their widows keep the pensions.
10. Knife-Jaw Joren — pit fighter with sahuagin teeth sewn into his gums; undefeated, possibly no longer entirely human.
11. The Lamplighter — tends the reef beacons; the only man the sahuagin won't take, and nobody knows why.
12. Widow Salt — brews brine-poison for assassinations; each batch named after the husband it avenged.
13. Quartermaster Voss — sells imperial weapons out the back of the garrison; checks every coin by biting it twice.
14. Little Fen — nine-year-old pickpocket who claims she can hold her breath for an hour; the guild is testing the claim.
15. Anatomist Dr. Morwain — pays in silver for sahuagin corpses, in gold for live ones; her cellar stairs go down past the waterline.
16. Chaplain Brack — garrison priest who blesses the tide-gates each dawn; the one morning he slept late, the east gate drowned.
17. Sahuagin brood-tender Hysh — captured egg-guardian; sings to an empty tank and refuses all food but live crabs.
18. Ropemaker Tam — his nooses never rot; every gallows rope in the city is his work, and he measures necks for free.
19. The Drowned Rat's cook, Stove — feeds the crew things pulled from nets that no menu names; the crew has stopped asking.
20. Toll-keeper Agda — exacts the bridge toll in teeth since coin ran short; wears the defaults on a necklace.
21. Cartographer Ilya — maps the streets that only exist at low tide; three of her maps show a cathedral nobody built.
22. Bellfounder Orm — casting a bell meant to be heard underwater; the foundry works nights and the neighbors have moved.
23. Leechwife Nessa — her leeches refuse noble blood but gorge on sailors; she charges accordingly and keeps no records.
24. Deserter Corvin — imperial marine who swam away from a sahuagin boarding action; the Empire lists him drowned, the sahuagin list him property.
25. The Cistern King — rules the freshwater black market from the old reservoir; executes thieves by thirst, not hanging.
26. Gull-tongue Paz — interpreter who claims fluency in sahuagin clicks; both sides suspect she invents the translations for profit.
27. Shipbreaker Hedd — dismantles quarantined hulls plank by plank; wears a dead ship's bell as a helmet and answers to it.
28. Novice Sarella — fled the Salt Abbey with the pickling ledgers; half the hospice's "dead" are listed as inventory, alive.
29. Wreck-diver Sim — free-dives the sunken cathedral for relics; came up last week speaking a language his mother understands.
30. The Maw's Voice — a sahuagin priest broadcasting surrender terms through a drowned choir of captives; the terms get worse weekly.

## Places

- P1. The Rotting Pier — lower docks built on drowned tenements; at low tide you can hear the old city through the planks.
- P2. The Brine Chapel — salt-stained chapel in the pier pilings where Tidecaller Wren reads flotsam; entry costs a secret.
- P3. The Drowned Rat (tavern) — smugglers' den in a half-sunk hulk; the floor tilts with the tide and so do the deals.
- P4. The Salt Abbey hospice — whitewashed walls, vinegar smell, and ledgers that list the living as stores.
- P5. The Old Reservoir — freshwater fortress of the Cistern King; the last clean water in the city, guarded by the thirsty.
- P6. The Reef Beacons — lamplighter-tended lights marking the safe channel; one beacon shines black at new moon.
- P7. The Gallows Bridge — Agda's toll bridge; the railings are hung with the teeth of those who couldn't pay.
- P8. Morwain's Cellar — anatomist's surgery below the waterline; the tanks breathe when the tide comes in.
- P9. The Sunken Cathedral — visible only at lowest tide; Sim dives it, and something down there has started diving back.
- P10. The Garrison Tide-Gates — imperial engineering holding back the harbor; Chaplain Brack's blessing is load-bearing.
- P11. The Eel Market — Mirella's fencing stalls among the fish guts; everything for sale was worn by someone first.
- P12. The Quarantine Hulks — Hedd's shipbreaking yard of plague ships; the quarantine flags never come down, by design.

## Factions

- F1. The Blackwater Compact — Harlow's smuggler syndicate; owns the night reef and rents to anyone the Empire won't.
- F2. The Drowned Choir — the Maw's Voice's captive congregation; sings the surrender terms whether it wills or no.
- F3. The Brine Sisters — Salt Abbess's hospice order; pickles, prays, and keeps the inventory ledgers Sarella stole.
- F4. The Imperial Garrison — holds the tide-gates and the bridge; underpaid, undermanned, and selling its own armory.
- F5. The Freshwater Trust — Cistern King's monopoly; water is currency, thirst is enforcement.
- F6. The Lowtide Guild — Fen's pickpockets and Ilya's cartographers; maps what the tide uncovers, steals what it leaves.
- F7. The Reefwardens — the Lamplighter's old order, down to one man; keeps lights the city stopped paying for.
- F8. The Deep Maw's Brood — sahuagin priest caste pressing from below; even its defectors fear what it has become.

## Notes-A (rung 10)

A quarantine hulk broke its moorings in last night's storm and now blocks the safe channel; Hedd claims salvage rights, Harlow claims the cargo, and something aboard has been knocking in patterns since dawn.

## Notes-B (rung 25)

The east tide-gate's blessing lapsed three nights running — Brack swears he spoke it, the gate-wardens swear he didn't, and the lower wards have taken water each time. The Cistern King has tripled the freshwater price and blames imperial neglect; Voss is quietly selling gate-iron as scrap.

## Notes-C (rung 50)

Sarella's stolen ledgers name twelve "pickled" citizens whose families still pay hospice tithe; the Brine Sisters want the girl alive and the book burned. Paz has offered to parley with the Brood using terms she will not show either side, and Corvin has recognized one of the Drowned Choir's voices as his former lieutenant.
